A 911 call starts after someone sees the impact, finds a phone, and describes a location. Ghost-V2X knows the coordinates before the impact, because it saw the trajectories converge. When risk goes HIGH it already holds the exact lat/long, the cross streets, and how many people are in the roadway.

That does not dispatch an ambulance on a prediction, which would be wrong. It means when the call does come, dispatch already has a corroborating location and a headcount instead of a description. In cardiac and haemorrhagic trauma, minutes are the whole game.

Cities buying pedestrian safety today mostly buy sensors — roadside LiDAR and radar units mounted at each intersection, the approach behind most "smart intersection" programmes internationally. It works, and it costs a capital line item per corner plus power, mounting, and maintenance.

New York already installed the sensors. There are 968 DOT cameras, and 619 of them look at street intersections. The gap was never hardware, it was that nobody was reading them in real time. Every recommendation below comes from NYPD's own recorded contributing factor at that corner, matched to the countermeasure NYC DOT already deploys for it.
